### 2.8.1 Q-LEARNING
**[RL TABULAR](0%20-%20hot%20to%20use.md#essential-acronym-glossary) [OFF-POLICY] [VALUE-BASED] [DISCRETE ACTIONS]**

**LEVEL 1: TRIAGE**
* **Input / Output:** Input: Current state. Output: Q-Value (expected utility) for each possible action in that state.
* **GO (Use when):** Completely deterministic environments with very small, discrete state and action spaces (e.g. simple mazes, tic-tac-toe). Problems modelable as finite MDPs (Markov Decision Processes).
* **NO-GO (Avoid when):** The state space is continuous or enormous (e.g. millimeter spatial coordinates, screen pixels). The “Curse of Dimensionality” would exhaust RAM instantly.

**LEVEL 2: OPERATIONS**
* **Principles of operation:**
  * **Q-Table:** Keeps an exact matrix [States x Actions] in memory that is constantly updated.
  * **Bellman Equation:** Updates the value of a state-action pair by combining the immediate reward with the maximum expected value in the next state (Temporal Difference, TD).
  * **Off-Policy:** The algorithm learns the optimal policy (“what you would do at best”) independently of the exploration policy in use (which may be taking random moves).

| PROS | CONS |
| :--- | :--- |
| Mathematical guarantee of convergence to the optimal policy (if every state is visited infinitely often). | Inapplicable in the real world due to combinatorial explosion of states. |
| Completely interpretable: the Q-Table can be inspected directly. | No generalization capability (two visually identical states for one pixel are separate memory cells). |

**LEVEL 3: DEEP DIVE & TROUBLESHOOTING**
* **Key Hyperparameters:**
  * `alpha` (Learning Rate): How much new calculations overwrite previous memory.
  * `gamma` (Discount Factor): `0` = extreme myopia (values only immediate reward). `0.99` = long-term view (values the entire reward chain).
  * `epsilon` (Exploration Rate): Controls Epsilon-Greedy logic (probability of choosing a purely random move instead of the best known one to discover new strategies).
* **Update Function:** Temporal Difference (TD) Error.
* **Common Pitfalls & Quick Fixes:**
  * **Pitfall:** The algorithm runs forever and the table never converges.
    * *Quick Fix:* The state space is too fragmented. Apply severe discretization (binning) (e.g. group continuous speeds into "low/medium/high" intervals) to reduce Q-Table rows, or migrate directly to DQN.

### 2.8.2 DQN (Deep Q-Network)
**[DEEP RL](0%20-%20hot%20to%20use.md#essential-acronym-glossary) [OFF-POLICY] [VALUE-BASED] [DISCRETE ACTIONS]**

**LEVEL 1: TRIAGE**
* **Input / Output:** Input: Continuous, high-dimensional state (e.g. pixel tensor). Output: Q-Values for a discrete set of actions (e.g. [Up, Down, Jump, Shoot]).
* **GO (Use when):** Classic video games (Atari), logistics control with visual input. Huge state spaces but actions are strictly discrete and limited.
* **NO-GO (Avoid when):** Continuous action spaces (e.g. millimeter steering angle of a car, torque force of a robotic arm). DQN would require brutal discretization of actions, losing precision.

![Q-Learning and Deep-Q-Learning](../pictures/qlearning-deepq.png)

**LEVEL 2: OPERATIONS**
* **Principles of operation:**
  * Replaces the Q-Table with a neural network (e.g. [CNN](0%20-%20hot%20to%20use.md#essential-acronym-glossary) for pixels) mapping state to Q-Values.
  * **Experience Replay:** The agent saves past transitions `[State, Action, Reward, Next State]` in a buffer (memory). During training, it samples random mini-batches from the buffer to break the strong temporal correlation of sequential data.
  * **Target Network:** Uses a cloned network that updates very slowly to compute the target of the Bellman Equation, stabilizing training (avoids the “dog chasing its own tail” problem).

| PROS | CONS |
| :--- | :--- |
| Handles native complex inputs (images, sensor arrays) thanks to [CNN](0%20-%20hot%20to%20use.md#essential-acronym-glossary)/[MLP](0%20-%20hot%20to%20use.md#essential-acronym-glossary). | Chronically suffers from “Overestimation Bias” (tends to unrealistically overestimate Q-Values). |
| Very sample-efficient thanks to reuse of data in the Replay Buffer. | Hardware setup is bulky (requires [GPU](0%20-%20hot%20to%20use.md#essential-acronym-glossary) for network training on buffered data). |

**LEVEL 3: DEEP DIVE & TROUBLESHOOTING**
* **Key Hyperparameters:**
  * `buffer_size`: Capacity of the Experience Replay. If too small, the model “forgets” rare experiences.
  * `target_update_frequency`: How often to copy weights from the main network to the Target Network.
* **Typical Loss Function:** Huber Loss or [MSE](0%20-%20hot%20to%20use.md#essential-acronym-glossary) applied to the TD Error.
* **Common Pitfalls & Quick Fixes:**
  * **Pitfall:** Q-values explode to infinity during training and loss becomes NaN.
    * *Quick Fix:* Overestimation bias is out of control. Switch obligatorily to the **Double DQN** (DDQN) architecture, which decouples action selection from value evaluation, eliminating the chronic mathematical overestimation of standard Q-learning.

### 2.8.3 PPO (Proximal Policy Optimization)
**[DEEP RL](0%20-%20hot%20to%20use.md#essential-acronym-glossary) [ON-POLICY] [ACTOR-CRITIC] [SOTA BASELINE](0%20-%20hot%20to%20use.md#essential-acronym-glossary)**

**LEVEL 1: TRIAGE**
* **Input / Output:** Input: State. Output: Action probabilities (discrete) or means/variances (continuous) from the Actor + State value (Critic).
* **GO (Use when):** De facto standard baseline in industry. Continuous robotics control, [LLM](0%20-%20hot%20to%20use.md#essential-acronym-glossary) fine-tuning (RLHF - Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback), complex video games (e.g. Dota 2). When you want a robust algorithm that works immediately with few numerical crashes.
* **NO-GO (Avoid when):** Collecting samples in the environment is too expensive (e.g. fragile hardware robotics or very slow physical simulations). PPO is incredibly hungry for fresh data.

**LEVEL 2: OPERATIONS**
* **Principles of operation:**
  * **Actor-Critic:** Maintains two networks. The *Actor* decides what to do (the Policy). The *Critic* judges how good the current state is (Value function) to help the Actor correct itself.
  * **On-Policy:** The algorithm learns only from data collected by its current incarnation. After each weight update, old data are thrown away (no long-term Replay Buffer).
  * **Clipped objective function (Proximal):** The core of PPO. Mathematically prevents the new Policy from moving too far from the old one (clipping ratio), ensuring smooth updates and preventing catastrophic performance collapses in a single step.

| PROS | CONS |
| :--- | :--- |
| Extremely stable and almost insensitive to hyperparameter initialization. | Dramatic sample inefficiency (throws away data after using it). |
| Natively handles both continuous and discrete spaces. | Risk of premature convergence to local minima if exploration is not forced. |

**LEVEL 3: DEEP DIVE & TROUBLESHOOTING**
* **Key Hyperparameters:**
  * `clip_ratio` (Epsilon): Usually 0.2. Defines the maximum tolerated deviation per update.
  * `entropy_coef`: Very critical. Adds a bonus to the Loss if the network explores unexpected actions (fights deterministic collapse).
  * `gae_lambda` (Generalized Advantage Estimation): Regulates the bias-variance trade-off in the calculation of action advantages.
* **Typical Loss Function:** Sum of clipped Policy Loss, Value Function Loss ([MSE](0%20-%20hot%20to%20use.md#essential-acronym-glossary)), minus Entropy.
* **Common Pitfalls & Quick Fixes:**
  * **Pitfall:** The agent learns a safe but mediocre strategy in the first 10 minutes and then stops exploring completely (flat Loss, stagnant Reward).
    * *Quick Fix:* The Actor became hyper-safe too early (probability of the main action near 100%). Increase `entropy_coef` (e.g. from 0.01 to 0.05) to force the system to keep curiosity alive.

### 2.8.4 SAC (Soft Actor-Critic)
**[DEEP RL](0%20-%20hot%20to%20use.md#essential-acronym-glossary) [OFF-POLICY] [ACTOR-CRITIC] [MAXIMUM ENTROPY]**

**LEVEL 1: TRIAGE**
* **Input / Output:** Input: State. Output: Action in continuous space + Q-values.
* **GO (Use when):** Robotics (including real hardware), complex simulated locomotion (Mujoco, PyBullet). The environment requires optimizing every interaction while saving data. Excels in continuous domains.
* **NO-GO (Avoid when):** Strictly discrete action spaces (e.g. classic video games), although discrete variants exist; DQN or PPO are usually more direct setups for the discrete domain.

**LEVEL 2: OPERATIONS**
* **Principles of operation:**
  * **Maximum Entropy [RL](0%20-%20hot%20to%20use.md#essential-acronym-glossary):** It optimizes not only for reward maximization, but explicitly for maximizing the sum “Reward + Entropy (Chaos/Unpredictability).” This pushes the agent to find multiple valid successful strategies, making it highly robust.
  * **Off-Policy with Actor-Critic:** Combines the best of both worlds. Uses a Replay Buffer (like DQN) to be extremely sample-efficient, but uses an Actor-Critic structure (like PPO) to manage continuous actions.
  * **Twin Critics:** Uses two separate Critic networks and always takes the minimum predicted value between the two to crush the chronic Overestimation Bias of Q-learning domains at the root.

| PROS | CONS |
| :--- | :--- |
| Exceptional sample efficiency: perfect if the simulator or robot is slow. | Requires a lot of math under the hood (uses 3 or 5 neural networks simultaneously). |
| Finds highly robust policies resilient to environmental perturbations. | Sensitive to reward scaling (the reward/entropy trade-off requires balancing). |

**LEVEL 3: DEEP DIVE & TROUBLESHOOTING**
* **Key Hyperparameters:**
  * `alpha` (Temperature): Regulates the importance of entropy. Modern implementations optimize it automatically as a learned variable (Auto-Alpha).
  * `tau` (Polyak Averaging): Speed of soft update of target networks (e.g. 0.005).
* **Common Pitfalls & Quick Fixes:**
  * **Pitfall:** Unstable performance that degrades rapidly. The system struggles to balance the objective and exploration.
    * *Quick Fix:* Reward scaling is vital in SAC. Since the system optimizes `Reward + Alpha * Entropy`, if your environmental rewards range from 0 to 1,000,000 (and entropy is 1), entropy is ignored. Always scale rewards so their magnitude is comparable to the entropy term, or ensure the implementation is using automatic `alpha` adaptation.
