### 2.3.1 CLUSTERING: K-MEANS & DBSCAN
**[UNSUPERVISED] [TABULAR / LATENT] [CLUSTERING] [ANOMALY DETECTION]**

**LEVEL 1: TRIAGE**
* **Input / Output:** Input: Strictly normalized numerical vectors or **Latent Spaces extracted from other models**. Output: Cluster ID for each point (and an "outlier" flag for DBSCAN).
* **GO (Use when):** Segmentation of tabular data. **Deep Clustering Pipeline:** applied on the bottleneck output of an Autoencoder or UMAP to group complex data (images, time series).
* **NO-GO (Avoid when):** Direct inputs with very high dimensionality (raw images, raw text) without prior reduction (distances lose mathematical meaning).

![K-Means Clustering](../pictures/kmeans.png)

![K-Means vs DBSCAN Clustering](../pictures/kmeans-dbscan.jpg)

**LEVEL 2: OPERATIONS**
* **Principles of operation:**
  * **K-Means (Centroid-based):** Initializes `K` centroids, assigns each point to the nearest centroid, recalculates centroids as the mean of the points, iterates until convergence (minimization of intra-cluster variance).
  * **DBSCAN (Density-based):** Finds high-density core samples and aggregates nearby points. Isolated points in low-density areas are labeled as noise/anomalies (-1).

| ALGORITHM | PROS | CONS |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **K-Means** | Very fast, scales well on large datasets. | Requires defining `K` a priori. Assumes spherical clusters of similar density. Sensitive to outliers. |
| **DBSCAN** | Does not require defining the number of clusters. Finds arbitrary-shaped clusters. Isolates anomalies natively. | Very inflexible if clusters have very different densities. Sensitive to the Curse of Dimensionality. |

**LEVEL 3: DEEP DIVE & TROUBLESHOOTING**
* **Key Hyperparameters:**
  * **K-Means:** `n_clusters` (K). To find it: Elbow Method on inertia, or maximization of the Silhouette Score.
  * **DBSCAN:** `eps` (Local search radius), `min_samples` (Minimum points to define a dense region).
* **Common Pitfalls & Quick Fixes:**
  * **Pitfall:** K-Means returns completely different results on every run.
    * *Quick Fix:* Random initialization fell into local minima. Use `init='k-means++'` (space out the initial centroids).
  * **Pitfall:** DBSCAN merges the entire dataset into one huge cluster.
    * *Quick Fix:* The `eps` hyperparameter is too high. Reduce `eps` or increase `min_samples`. Use a *k-distance graph* to find the optimal `eps` (point of maximum curvature).

### 2.3.2 DIMENSIONALITY REDUCTION: [PCA](0%20-%20hot%20to%20use.md#essential-acronym-glossary), t-SNE, UMAP
**[UNSUPERVISED] [DATA TRANSFORMATION] [VISUALIZATION]**

**LEVEL 1: TRIAGE**
* **Input / Output:** Input: High-dimensional matrix. Output: Compressed low-dimensional matrix (usually 2D/3D for visualization or N-D for downstream training).
* **GO (Use when):** [PCA](0%20-%20hot%20to%20use.md#essential-acronym-glossary): Removal of multicollinearity, reduction of computational load before other [ML](0%20-%20hot%20to%20use.md#essential-acronym-glossary) models. t-SNE/UMAP: 2D visual exploration of highly complex datasets (e.g. textual embeddings or genomics).
* **NO-GO (Avoid when):** t-SNE should NEVER be used to generate features for a predictive model (it does not preserve global distances).

![Dimensionality Reduction](../pictures/dim-reduction.png)

**LEVEL 2: OPERATIONS**
* **Principles of operation:**
  * **[PCA](0%20-%20hot%20to%20use.md#essential-acronym-glossary) (Linear):** Orthogonal projection that seeks the axes (Principal Components) where data variance is maximal.
  * **t-SNE (Non-Linear):** Converts distances into probabilities. Tries to keep nearby points in the 2D space close to what they were in the original space. Ignores global structure.
  * **UMAP (Non-Linear / Topological):** Builds a graph in the original space and optimizes it in the low-dimensional space. It is the successor to t-SNE.

| ALGORITHM | PROS | CONS |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **[PCA](0%20-%20hot%20to%20use.md#essential-acronym-glossary)** | Fast, deterministic, preserves global structure. Components are orthogonal. | Does not capture non-linear relationships. Sensitive to outliers. |
| **t-SNE** | Excellent at visually separating clusters. | Slow, stochastic (changes every run), destroys global distance proportions. |
| **UMAP** | Faster than t-SNE, preserves topology better, can do useful feature extraction for [ML](0%20-%20hot%20to%20use.md#essential-acronym-glossary). | Requires meticulous tuning; the underlying math is complex (differential geometry). |

**LEVEL 3: DEEP DIVE & TROUBLESHOOTING**
* **Key Hyperparameters:**
  * **[PCA](0%20-%20hot%20to%20use.md#essential-acronym-glossary):** `n_components` (Select based on cumulative explained variance, e.g. 95%).
  * **t-SNE:** `perplexity` (Balances local and global neighborhood attention, typically between 5 and 50).
  * **UMAP:** `n_neighbors` (High = focus on global structure; Low = focus on local details), `min_dist` (How tightly points can compact).
* **Common Pitfalls & Quick Fixes:**
  * **Pitfall:** The first [PCA](0%20-%20hot%20to%20use.md#essential-acronym-glossary) component dominates with 99% of the variance, but the data make no sense.
    * *Quick Fix:* You did not scale the data. [PCA](0%20-%20hot%20to%20use.md#essential-acronym-glossary) seeks maximum absolute variance: a feature measured in “Millions” will crush a feature measured in “Percentages”. `StandardScaler` is mandatory.
  * **Pitfall:** Clusters generated by t-SNE all seem the same size and separated by equal distances.
    * *Quick Fix:* This is an optical artifact of t-SNE: in this algorithm the cluster size and distance between different clusters in the 2D space have no metric meaning. Do not draw analytical conclusions; use UMAP to preserve distances.

### 2.3.3 AUTOENCODERS (STANDARD & [VAE](0%20-%20hot%20to%20use.md#essential-acronym-glossary))
**[UNSUPERVISED] [DEEP LEARNING] [REPRESENTATION] [ANOMALY DETECTION]**

**LEVEL 1: TRIAGE**
* **Input / Output:** Input: Images, time signals, or dense vectors. Output: 1. Reconstruction of the input. 2. **Compressed latent vector (Bottleneck)**.
* **GO (Use when):** Denoising of images/signals. Anomaly Detection (high reconstruction error on unseen data). **Feature Extraction (Deep Clustering):** use the Bottleneck as input for classical models (K-Means/[SVM](0%20-%20hot%20to%20use.md#essential-acronym-glossary)) or UMAP, allowing classical [ML](0%20-%20hot%20to%20use.md#essential-acronym-glossary) to digest unstructured data.
* **NO-GO (Avoid when):** Simple, small tabular datasets ([PCA](0%20-%20hot%20to%20use.md#essential-acronym-glossary) is faster, more interpretable, and less prone to overfitting).

![Autoencoder Architecture](../pictures/autoencoders.png)

**LEVEL 2: OPERATIONS**
* **Principles of operation:**
  * **Hourglass Architecture:** Composed of an **Encoder** (reduces dimensionality to a “Bottleneck” or Latent Space) and a **Decoder** (tries to reconstruct the input using only the Bottleneck).
  * **[VAE](0%20-%20hot%20to%20use.md#essential-acronym-glossary) (Variational Autoencoder):** Instead of mapping an input to a single latent point, it maps it to a probability distribution (mean and variance). This forces the latent space to be continuous, allowing sampling of new invented points (Generation).

| MODEL | PROS | CONS |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Standard AE** | Excellent for denoising and deterministic feature extraction. | The latent space is irregular and “empty” in many points: not suitable for generating new data. |
| **[VAE](0%20-%20hot%20to%20use.md#essential-acronym-glossary)** | Continuous and structured latent space. Enables generation, interpolation between images/data, and sampling. | Reconstructions are often blurrier than GANs or Diffusion Models. Training is complex. |

**LEVEL 3: DEEP DIVE & TROUBLESHOOTING**
* **Key Hyperparameters:**
  * Network Architecture (Bottleneck size, number of layers).
  * **[VAE](0%20-%20hot%20to%20use.md#essential-acronym-glossary):** Weight of the [KL](0%20-%20hot%20to%20use.md#essential-acronym-glossary) Divergence (Beta). Balances reconstruction fidelity (Reconstruction Loss) with latent-space regularization.
* **Typical Loss Function:** [MSE](0%20-%20hot%20to%20use.md#essential-acronym-glossary) or [BCE](0%20-%20hot%20to%20use.md#essential-acronym-glossary) for reconstruction (Standard AE). [MSE](0%20-%20hot%20to%20use.md#essential-acronym-glossary) + [KL](0%20-%20hot%20to%20use.md#essential-acronym-glossary) Divergence ([VAE](0%20-%20hot%20to%20use.md#essential-acronym-glossary)).
* **Common Pitfalls & Quick Fixes:**
  * **Pitfall (Standard AE):** The Autoencoder has zero loss but does not learn useful features (it returns the input identically).
    * *Quick Fix:* The bottleneck is too wide or the network is too capable. The AE is acting like an “Identity Function” (simple copy-paste). Reduce bottleneck neurons drastically or add noise to the input (Denoising AE) to force generalization.
  * **Pitfall ([VAE](0%20-%20hot%20to%20use.md#essential-acronym-glossary)):** “Posterior Collapse” or [KL](0%20-%20hot%20to%20use.md#essential-acronym-glossary) Vanishing. The encoder ignores the input data and always predicts a standard Gaussian. The decoder collapses, generating the same mean every time.
    * *Quick Fix:* The [KL](0%20-%20hot%20to%20use.md#essential-acronym-glossary) loss is dominating too early in training. Apply “[KL](0%20-%20hot%20to%20use.md#essential-acronym-glossary) Annealing”: set the [KL](0%20-%20hot%20to%20use.md#essential-acronym-glossary) loss weight to 0 at the beginning of training and increase it linearly toward 1 over the epochs, allowing the decoder to learn reconstruction first.
