### 2.7.1 [TF-IDF](0%20-%20hot%20to%20use.md#essential-acronym-glossary) & NAIVE BAYES
**[SUPERVISED] [TEXT] [BASELINE] [BAG-OF-WORDS]**

**LEVEL 1: TRIAGE**
* **Input / Output:** Input: Corpus of textual documents. Output: Sparse matrix ([TF-IDF](0%20-%20hot%20to%20use.md#essential-acronym-glossary)) -> class probability (Naive Bayes).
* **GO (Use when):** Absolute and immediate baseline for long-text classification (e.g. spam detection, basic sentiment analysis). Small datasets. When compute resources are close to zero.
* **NO-GO (Avoid when):** The meaning of the text depends on word order, sarcasm, or complex negations (e.g. “It is not true that the movie was bad”). The Bag-of-Words approach destroys the sequential context completely.

**LEVEL 2: OPERATIONS**
* **Principles of operation:**
  * **[TF-IDF](0%20-%20hot%20to%20use.md#essential-acronym-glossary):** Vectorizes text. Assigns a high weight to words frequent in a single document (Term Frequency) but rare in the entire corpus (Inverse Document Frequency), penalizing articles and prepositions.
  * **Naive Bayes:** Probabilistic algorithm based on Bayes’ Theorem. Assumes (naively) that the presence of one word is statistically independent of the others.

| PROS | CONS |
| :--- | :--- |
| Training and inference speed is lightning-fast on [CPU](0%20-%20hot%20to%20use.md#essential-acronym-glossary). | Zero semantic understanding: “dog bites man” and “man bites dog” have the exact same vector. |
| Total interpretability: you can print the weight of every single word. | Generates extremely sparse matrices (millions of zeros) that waste RAM if not handled properly. |

**LEVEL 3: DEEP DIVE & TROUBLESHOOTING**
* **Key Hyperparameters:**
  * **[TF-IDF](0%20-%20hot%20to%20use.md#essential-acronym-glossary):** `ngram_range` (use bigrams, e.g. (1,2), helps capture negations like “not good”), `min_df` / `max_df` (remove words too rare or too common to reduce sparsity).
  * **Naive Bayes:** `alpha` (Laplace Smoothing): prevents a word never seen in training from driving the probability of the entire sentence to zero.
* **Common Pitfalls & Quick Fixes:**
  * **Pitfall:** Immediate Out Of Memory ([OOM](0%20-%20hot%20to%20use.md#essential-acronym-glossary)) during [TF-IDF](0%20-%20hot%20to%20use.md#essential-acronym-glossary) fit on medium datasets.
    * *Quick Fix:* Do not convert the output to a dense NumPy array (`.toarray()`). Keep the format strictly as `scipy.sparse`. If necessary, lower `max_features` to 10,000-20,000.

### 2.7.2 TRANSFORMER ENCODERS ([BERT](0%20-%20hot%20to%20use.md#essential-acronym-glossary), RoBERTa)
**[SUPERVISED / PRE-TRAINED] [TEXT] [EMBEDDING] [CLASSIFICATION]**

**LEVEL 1: TRIAGE**
* **Input / Output:** Input: Token sequence. Output: Dense vector (semantic embedding) or classification logits through a dense head applied to the special `[CLS]` token.
* **GO (Use when):** Named Entity Recognition (NER), complex sentiment analysis, semantic search engines (creating vectors for vector databases). When deep and bidirectional understanding of the text is required.
* **NO-GO (Avoid when):** You need to generate new text or translate. Encoders “read” and “understand,” but do not “write” fluently and auto-regressively.

**LEVEL 2: OPERATIONS**
* **Principles of operation:**
  * **Bidirectionality:** Unlike RNNs that read left-to-right, self-attention mechanisms allow each token to view the entire context (right and left) simultaneously.
  * **Masked Language Modeling (MLM):** During pre-training (on terabytes of text), 15% of words are hidden. The network learns syntax and semantics by trying to guess the missing words.
  * During Fine-Tuning, the pre-trained weights are kept and only a final classification layer is trained for a few epochs.

| PROS | CONS |
| :--- | :--- |
| They understand context extremely well (solve syntactic ambiguities). | Strict input length limits (typically 512 tokens due to quadratic memory constraints). |
| [SOTA](0%20-%20hot%20to%20use.md#essential-acronym-glossary) performance on almost all NLU classification tasks. | Require [GPU](0%20-%20hot%20to%20use.md#essential-acronym-glossary) for reasonable fine-tuning times. |

**LEVEL 3: DEEP DIVE & TROUBLESHOOTING**
* **Key Hyperparameters:**
  * `learning_rate`: Must be very low (e.g. 2e-5, 3e-5) so as not to destroy the pre-trained weights.
  * `max_length`: Padding/truncation length. Increasing it quickly saturates [VRAM](0%20-%20hot%20to%20use.md#essential-acronym-glossary).
* **Typical Loss Function:** Cross-Entropy.
* **Common Pitfalls & Quick Fixes:**
  * **Pitfall:** Catastrophic Forgetting. Training loss drops to zero, but the model becomes poor in validation and loses its original linguistic capability.
    * *Quick Fix:* Learning rate is too high or you trained for too many epochs (usually 2-4 epochs are enough for [BERT](0%20-%20hot%20to%20use.md#essential-acronym-glossary) fine-tuning). Use a scheduler with warm-up and linear decay.

### 2.7.3 TRANSFORMER DECODERS (LLMs: Llama, Qwen, etc.)
**[SELF-SUPERVISED] [TEXT] [GENERATION] [RAG] [AGENTS]**

**LEVEL 1: TRIAGE**
* **Input / Output:** Input: Text prompt (often structured with system instructions). Output: Subsequent tokens generated auto-regressively.
* **GO (Use when):** RAG systems (Retrieval-Augmented Generation), conversational agents, extraction of unstructured data via complex prompts, or local coding assistant backends (e.g. via VS Code/Continue.dev integration with models like Qwen hosted on Ollama and running on WSL2).
* **NO-GO (Avoid when):** Massive binary classification (too slow and expensive compared to RoBERTa). Tasks requiring absolute mathematical determinism without external validation (the hallucination risk is architecturally inescapable).

**LEVEL 2: OPERATIONS**
* **Principles of operation:**
  * **Auto-Regressive:** Predict strictly one token at a time from left to right (Next-Token Prediction).
  * **KV-Cache:** To avoid recalculating attention matrices over past tokens at each new generation step, they save intermediate computations in memory space (Key-Value Cache).
  * **Alignment:** After pre-training, they are optimized via SFT (Supervised Fine-Tuning) and RLHF/DPO to follow human instructions instead of merely completing phrases from the internet.

| PROS | CONS |
| :--- | :--- |
| Zero/Few-shot learning: they adapt to new tasks without retraining (Prompt Engineering). | Enormous [VRAM](0%20-%20hot%20to%20use.md#essential-acronym-glossary) consumption, exacerbated linearly by longer contexts via the KV-Cache. |
| Extreme versatility (they write code, summarize, reason through logical steps). | Subject to hallucinations. Generation latency is high and depends on output length. |

**LEVEL 3: DEEP DIVE & TROUBLESHOOTING**
* **Key Hyperparameters in Inference:**
  * `temperature`: Controls stochasticity. `0.0` (or `0.1` for coding) = Deterministic, chooses the most probable token. `0.7-1.0` = Creative, introduces variance.
  * `top_p` (Nucleus Sampling): Cuts the long tail of improbable tokens. A better alternative to Top-K.
  * Quantization (e.g. GGUF formats at 4-bit/8-bit): Vital to host the model on consumer GPUs while losing only a negligible fraction of performance.
* **Common Pitfalls & Quick Fixes:**
  * **Pitfall:** Out of Memory ([OOM](0%20-%20hot%20to%20use.md#essential-acronym-glossary)) during generation of very long answers or in extended chat sessions.
    * *Quick Fix:* The KV-Cache exploded. You need to limit `max_new_tokens` or actively clear historical context by passing only the last N turns of the system.
  * **Pitfall:** Full-parameter fine-tuning impossible due to lack of [GPU](0%20-%20hot%20to%20use.md#essential-acronym-glossary) cluster.
    * *Quick Fix:* Use **LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation)**. Freeze the original model and train only lower-rank matrices stacked on top. Reduces trainable weights by 99%, allowing fine-tuning of 7B-14B models on single 24GB [VRAM](0%20-%20hot%20to%20use.md#essential-acronym-glossary) GPUs.

### 2.7.4 SEQUENCE-TO-SEQUENCE ([T5](0%20-%20hot%20to%20use.md#essential-acronym-glossary), BART)
**[SUPERVISED] [TEXT] [ENCODER-DECODER]**

**LEVEL 1: TRIAGE**
* **Input / Output:** Input: Text. Output: Text. Both can have drastically different lengths.
* **GO (Use when):** Purely transformative tasks: Machine Translation, Abstractive Text Summarization, grammar correction, style rewriting.
* **NO-GO (Avoid when):** Simple text classification (the Encoder is enough; the Decoder only adds unnecessary weight). Open-ended chat agents (Decoder-only LLMs dominate in this task).

**LEVEL 2: OPERATIONS**
* **Principles of operation:**
  * Combines both worlds. The **Encoder** processes the input bidirectionally (understanding the whole context). It then passes the semantic state (via Cross-Attention) to the **Decoder**, which generates the output auto-regressively (left to right).
  * **[T5](0%20-%20hot%20to%20use.md#essential-acronym-glossary) (Text-to-Text Transfer Transformer):** Reframes *every* [NLP](0%20-%20hot%20to%20use.md#essential-acronym-glossary) problem as text generation. For example, for classification, the input is “sentiment: the movie is awful” and [T5](0%20-%20hot%20to%20use.md#essential-acronym-glossary) does not return a logit `0`, but physically generates the text string “negative”.

| PROS | CONS |
| :--- | :--- |
| Excellent at aligning rich-context sources with complex outputs (like summaries). | Heavier architecture than individual Encoders or small Decoders. |
| Very flexible thanks to the [T5](0%20-%20hot%20to%20use.md#essential-acronym-glossary) text-to-text paradigm. | Often superseded by modern large LLMs for generality of use, unless specific fine-tuning is required. |

**LEVEL 3: DEEP DIVE & TROUBLESHOOTING**
* **Key Hyperparameters in Inference (Search Strategy):**
  * `num_beams` (Beam Search): Instead of always choosing the locally most probable word (Greedy), it explores N parallel branches of sentences and selects the globally most probable combination. Crucial for translation quality.
  * `length_penalty`: If > 1.0, it forces Beam Search to prefer longer sentences; if < 1.0 it favors shorter responses.
* **Common Pitfalls & Quick Fixes:**
  * **Pitfall:** Summaries generated repeat the same identical sentence in an infinite loop, forever.
    * *Quick Fix:* This is a known problem in basic auto-regressive generation. Activate and calibrate the `repetition_penalty` hyperparameter (e.g. 1.2) or use `no_repeat_ngram_size=2` (prevents generating the same bigram twice).
